Services

Most properties underperform not because of neglect, but because habitat, timber, and access decisions get made independently of each other. We look at how the pieces interact first, then figure out what to do, in what order, and what to leave alone.

Habitat & Hunt Layouts

Layout work starts on foot, not on a screen. We walk the property to understand how animals are actually using it — bedding, movement, forage, water — and how hunting pressure is affecting that behavior over time.

Entry and exit routes, stand locations, and habitat work all get planned together so the layout still functions after years of real use, not just the first season. Where applicable, we also make sure hunting goals don't conflict with future timber or access work.

What we look at

  • Prevailing and seasonal wind patterns
  • Bedding, forage, thermal cover, and security cover relationships
  • Human access, sightlines, and pressure distribution
  • Compatibility with timber operations or land leases

Timber Stand Improvement (TSI)

TSI is rarely about cutting more — it's about cutting the right things, at the right time, for the right reasons. A lot of New England woodlots have been high-graded or left unmanaged long enough that regeneration, species composition, and long-term value are already being shaped by past decisions, whether the owner knows it or not.

We evaluate stand conditions, past disturbance history, and your goals before recommending action. The aim is better health and growth while keeping future harvest options open — not trading tomorrow's value for a cleaner-looking woods today.

What we look at

  • Species composition and regeneration potential
  • Crop tree selection and release strategy
  • Access planning to protect soils and avoid long-term compaction
  • How thinning decisions affect future harvest flexibility

Land Stewardship Planning

Stewardship planning pulls habitat, timber, and access together into a sequence you can actually work through over time. It's not a document for a binder — it's a framework for making decisions as conditions change and priorities shift.

Just as important as what to do is what to leave alone. Identifying which actions to delay — and understanding why — is often where the most value is preserved. Plans are phased so early work doesn't foreclose later options.

What we focus on

  • Sequencing to avoid conflicts and rework
  • Capital timing and realistic execution windows
  • Tradeoffs between habitat, timber income, and access
  • Long-term resilience over short-term appearance

Supporting services

Depending on the property and the project, these often run alongside the core work.

Owner's Rep & Project Coordination

Some properties need more than planning — access improvements, timber harvest coordination, habitat implementation, or multiple specialists working in sequence. When that's the case, we can act as the landowner's representative: evaluating options, coordinating qualified professionals, and keeping work aligned as it moves from planning into execution.

May include

  • Access and road planning with licensed engineers and excavation contractors
  • Timber harvest oversight and sequencing
  • Habitat implementation coordination
  • Introductions for land leases — clubs, outfitters, ranges

Advisory and coordination only. Regulated work is performed by licensed and insured professionals under separate agreements.

Site Analysis & Mapping Support

Aerial imagery and terrain data are useful tools when they're tied to real field decisions — access placement, drainage risk, canopy structure, equipment feasibility. We use mapping to reduce guesswork on expensive calls, not to produce deliverables for their own sake.

What it supports

  • Terrain, slope, and drainage pattern review
  • Canopy structure and disturbance history
  • Access feasibility before equipment mobilizes
  • Decision support — not survey or permitting work

Legal boundary surveys and permitting are referred to licensed professionals.
