About 23 Timber Co.

23 Timber Co. is a land planning and aerial mapping practice built for private landowners, land buyers, and contractors working in northern New England. The work is practical by design — rooted in field experience, backed by GIS and LiDAR analysis, and focused on decisions that hold up over time, not just on paper.

The team

Two people with complementary skills and a shared bias toward getting the answer right before any work starts.

Randy Whitmore

Founder & Owner-Operator

I started 23 Timber because the people who own land in New England deserve someone who looks at the whole picture. A forester thinks about timber. A hunting consultant thinks about food plots. A logger thinks about what's coming down today. Nobody is thinking about how all of it connects — or what the property looks like in thirty years when the decisions being made right now have had time to play out.

I've spent thirty years hunting this ground and a career managing complex construction projects — coordinating specialists, sequencing work, and making sure decisions made early don't create problems later. That's exactly what good land management requires. Gather the right information, bring in the right people, and build a plan that holds up over time.

The goal is always the same: leave the land better than you found it, and leave the owner with something worth passing down.

How he works

I grew up around equipment, small farms, and woods that had stories in them if you knew how to read them. That didn't translate into a plan right away — it took years of hunting, burning, planting, and making mistakes on real ground before the pieces connected into something useful.

What I'm not interested in is drive-by consulting. I'd rather walk your land, understand what you actually have, and tell you what I think — including the parts that aren't what you were hoping to hear. That's the only version of this work that holds up.

Aerial operations over private land — especially timber and terrain work where the data feeds real decisions — require more than flying ability. They require the discipline to plan the mission properly, identify risk before it becomes a problem, and deliver a consistent result regardless of conditions. That's what Steven brings, and it's why the mapping work gets done right the first time.

What drives the work

The name comes from colonial history — specifically the practice of cutting white pine to 23 inches to stay just below the 24-inch threshold the British Crown had claimed for the Royal Navy. It wasn't symbolic. It was practical resistance by people who needed those trees to survive and weren't willing to hand them over to a distant authority that had no relationship with the land.

That tension — between people who live on the land and institutions that make decisions about it from a distance — hasn't gone away. 23 Timber Co. exists in the tradition of private landowners having informed agency over their property. Not as rebellion, but as a commitment to stewardship that's rooted in the ground, not in a policy document.
